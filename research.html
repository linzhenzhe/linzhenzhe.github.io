ACM MOBISYS 2026 · FIRST AUTHOR

WiNB: Learning-Driven Wideband 5G NR Backscatter

Learning-based receivers and efficient AI inference for real-world wireless systems

71 MbpsThroughput16 μsDemodulation / symbol~10³×Demodulation speedup

Research Challenge

Wideband OFDM backscatter promises high-throughput, low-power IoT connectivity, but conventional demodulation is computationally expensive, depends on accurate channel estimates, and is sensitive to synchronization errors.

Our Approach

WiNB introduces a dual-task transformer that learns channel estimation and backscatter bit decoding together. Sharing representations across these tasks improves decoding robustness and reduces the need for expensive iterative optimization. A tailored pruning framework further reduces inference latency.

Key Contributions

  • Joint learning architecture for channel estimation and backscatter demodulation.
  • Model pruning and inference optimization to approach practical OFDM symbol times.
  • End-to-end 28 GHz 5G NR backscatter prototype and cross-environment evaluation.

Results & Significance

The prototype achieves 71 Mbps at a reported BER of 10⁻⁴ and a 16 μs per-symbol demodulation time, approximately three orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based baselines. This project connects AI model design with real-time wireless systems and motivates my interest in efficient edge intelligence.

ACM SENSYS 2026 · CO-PRIMARY AUTHOR

B³: Bistatic Backscatter Beamforming for mmWave IoTs

Scalable, protocol-compatible beamforming for low-power mmWave tags

13 dBUp to SNR gain11 mCommunication range2.5 mWTag power

Research Challenge

Commodity mmWave backscatter suffers from weak reflected signals and beam alignment overhead. Passive tags cannot easily estimate their own channels or perform conventional beam-training feedback, especially with multiple tags.

Our Approach

B³ uses multibeam backscatter within standard 802.11ad/ay beamforming preambles. Pulse-position and on-off keying convey tag identity and channel information without changing the underlying protocol. The receiver isolates backscatter paths, estimates angles of departure, and derives beam weights for both ends of the bistatic link.

Key Contributions

  • Backscatter channel isolation from multipath and direct-path interference.
  • Angle-of-departure estimation and beam-weight optimization for high-gain links.
  • Concurrent multi-tag detection and beamforming within a single training round.

Results & Significance

Evaluations report up to 13 dB SNR improvement over codebook-based beamforming and communication up to 11 m at BER ≤ 10⁻³, including challenging blockage and NLoS scenarios. The 60 GHz tag consumes 2.5 mW.

USENIX NSDI 2024 · SECOND AUTHOR

mmComb: High-Speed mmWave Commodity Wi-Fi Backscatter

Integrating ultra-low-power IoT connectivity into existing 60 GHz networks

55 MbpsPeak data rate87.3 μWTag power>19 dBSINR improvement

Research Challenge

Earlier high-speed mmWave backscatter designs often required specialized readers or modifications to commodity hardware. Weak tag reflections also face strong self-interference from direct and multipath signals.

Our Approach

mmComb embeds tag data into existing IEEE 802.11ad/ay beamforming frames without changing AP/client hardware or protocol operation. Receive beamforming steers gain toward the tag while creating spatial nulls toward interfering paths.

Key Contributions

  • Protocol-compliant backscatter using commodity Wi-Fi control frames.
  • Spatial self-interference suppression using receive beamforming and nulling.
  • Custom 60 GHz tag and end-to-end evaluation with off-the-shelf devices.

Results & Significance

The prototype achieves 55 Mbps at 87.3 μW tag power, with BER below 10⁻³ at distances up to 5.5 m. Spatial suppression improves backscatter SINR by over 19 dB compared with commodity beams.
